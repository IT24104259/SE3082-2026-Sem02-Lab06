Without collapse - how threads are generated:
#pragma omp parallel for on the nested loop pair only parallelizes the outer loop (r, 5 iterations). OpenMP distributes the 5 values of r across the available threads, and whichever thread is assigned a given r value executes the entire inner loop (k, 10 iterations) for that r sequentially, by itself. This means the granularity of parallel work is limited to 5 units - with more than 5 threads, some threads receive no work at all, and load balancing depends entirely on how evenly the 5 r values divide among the threads.

With #pragma omp parallel for collapse(2):
collapse(2) merges the two nested loops into a single logical loop of 5 × 10 = 50 total iterations. OpenMP then distributes all 50 (r, k) combinations across the thread team, rather than only distributing the 5 outer-loop values. This produces much finer-grained and typically more balanced work distribution, and allows more threads to be usefully employed even when the outer loop alone has too few iterations to keep a large thread team busy. In the output, thread assignments were spread across many different (r, k) pairs rather than each thread "owning" one or two entire rows of the inner loop.
